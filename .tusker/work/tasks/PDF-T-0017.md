# PDF-T-0017 · Make decompression limits evidence-based and configurable

## Intent

- What this is: Allow legitimate large judgments to be processed without weakening the extractor's decompression-bomb defense.
- Why it matters: A normal 200-page Supreme Court judgment currently fails as if it were corrupt, leaving operators unable to distinguish a tunable capacity limit from a bad source document.
- What "done" looks like: The real document's expansion is measured, safe defaults are evidence-based and operator-configurable, limit failures have a distinct machine-readable reason, and a regression check proves the large judgment succeeds while the genuinely garbled control remains rejected by its existing quality gate.

## Acceptance

| ID | Outcome | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | The true decoded-stream total of the failing 2024 judgment and the large end of the available corpus are measured and reported, rather than inferred from the early-abort value. | Measurement report plus reproducible command. |
| A2 | Decompression limits use an evidence-based absolute and/or input-relative policy that admits legitimate judgments while retaining a finite bomb backstop. | Focused automated limit-policy tests and corpus measurements. |
| A3 | Operators can override the decompression policy at runtime without rebuilding, with defaults remaining safe and backward-compatible at the API boundary. | Configuration parsing/API tests. |
| A4 | A decompression-resource refusal is machine-distinguishable from corrupt or low-quality input. | Structured error classification tests. |
| A5 | A runnable document-bound regression check proves the 2024 judgment parses and the 1985 garbled scan remains refused without changing the quality gate. | Three-document regression command and output summary. |

## Non-goals

- Changing the parse-quality threshold or quarantine policy.
- Changing backend retry classification or job error mapping.
- Treating the one observed document as sufficient evidence for an unbounded constant increase.
